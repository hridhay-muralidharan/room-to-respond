# Room to Respond — mental workout model

This document defines the physical-workout parallel used across the product. It keeps the vocabulary familiar while making clear what each term means in Room to Respond.

## The central parallel

Physical gyms provide physical workouts for physical muscles. A workout has a goal, targets particular muscle groups, combines exercises, adjusts difficulty, and produces something the person can track.

Room to Respond provides mental workouts for mental faculties. A mental workout has a development goal, targets particular faculties, combines exercises around a moment from the client’s life, adjusts the challenge, and produces something the client and coach can review.

The parallel is an organising model. It does not claim that mental faculties behave exactly like muscles or that every exercise produces a measurable training effect.

## The vocabulary map

| Physical workout | Room to Respond | Meaning in the product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fitness goal | Development goal | What the client wants to become more capable of doing in moments from life. Example: create more room before responding to criticism. |
| Workout format | Mental workout format | The kind of capability being exercised. Initial formats include Pause, Perspective, Courage, and Flexibility. |
| Muscle group | Faculty group | The faculties involved in the workout. The current groups are Notice, Understand, Choose, and Adapt. |
| Muscle | Mental faculty | A specific capacity such as attention, emotion awareness, metacognition, judgment, communication, flexibility, foresight, or learning. |
| Workout of the Day (WOD) | Workout of the Moment | A short combination of exercises built around one moment from life and one development goal. |
| Exercise | Mental exercise | A specific action such as naming what happened, checking the first story, trying a changed condition, or rehearsing a sentence. |
| Movement | Prompt or task | The instruction that tells the client what to do now. |
| Set | Pass through a situation | One round of a prompt sequence using the same situation. |
| Rep | Response or observation | One completed response, comparison, correction, or rehearsal. |
| Weight or resistance | Situation difficulty | A condition that makes the exercise easier or harder: time pressure, ambiguity, emotional intensity, competing goals, limited support, or another person's response. |
| Form | Quality of attention and response | Whether the client can describe the moment specifically, separate observation from assumption, name what matters, and choose deliberately. This is observed in the work, not scored as a clinical measure. |
| Warm-up | Arrive at the situation | Bring a concrete moment into view and notice what is already happening before analysing it. |
| Main movement | Core exercise | The central reflection, variation, rehearsal, or decision task. |
| Cooldown | Integration | Name what changed, what stayed present, and what to carry into the next situation. |
| Trainer | Coach | Sets the direction, selects or adapts the routine, observes the work, and shapes the next workout. |
| Class plan | Routine | The ordered set of exercises the coach assigns to the client. |
| Scaled version | Adjusted challenge | The same exercise with more or less time, support, ambiguity, emotional distance, or complexity. |
| Full-body workout | Multi-faculty workout | A routine that follows a situation across several faculties rather than isolating one faculty. |
| Training programme | Development sequence | A series of routines that builds from what the coach and client learn over time. |
| Workout log | Client report | The client's account of what they did, noticed, corrected, and want to explore next. |
| Progress check | Review point | A coach-client discussion of what became more visible, more possible, or still difficult. |

## How Cult’s workout criteria translate

Cult describes a useful Workout of the Day as easy to learn, scalable, capable of targeting multiple muscle groups, and workable with minimal equipment. Its classes are trainer-led, varied, and structured with warm-up, main movements, and cooldown. ([How Cult designs workouts](https://blog.cult.fit/posts/how-we-design-our-workouts), [Cult workout formats](https://support.cult.fit/support/solutions/articles/25000006132-what-kind-of-workout-formats-are-available-at-cult-), [Cult beginner format](https://support.cult.fit/support/solutions/articles/25000017453-i-have-never-worked-out-before-from-where-should-i-begin-))

Room to Respond should apply the same design criteria:

### Easy to learn

The client should understand the prompt quickly and begin working with their own situation. The product should explain one action at a time using ordinary language.

### Scalable

The coach should be able to make the same workout gentler or more demanding. A beginner may start with a low-stakes moment, more time, and more support. A more experienced client may work with higher stakes, less time, competing needs, or a live conversation they need to prepare for.

### Multiple faculties

A situation can exercise several faculties in sequence. A workout about responding to criticism may involve:

- **Notice:** attention, emotion awareness, and bodily signals;
- **Understand:** memory, assumptions, and perspective;
- **Choose:** judgment, values, communication, and self-regulation;
- **Adapt:** flexibility, foresight, and learning.

The coach can emphasise one faculty while still allowing the workout to involve the others.

### Minimal equipment

The client needs a situation, their own words, and a clear prompt. GenAI provides adaptation and variation. It should not make the workout feel like the client needs to learn a new software system before beginning.

### Trainer-led

The coach chooses the development goal, selects the workout format, adjusts the challenge, reviews the reflection, and sets the next routine. The product supports the coach’s judgement.

### Varied

The same faculty should be exercised through different situations and exercises. Repeating “check the first story” word-for-word is not a training programme. The task should change while the underlying faculty remains relevant.

## The Room to Respond workout card

Every routine should be described using the same card structure:

1. **Workout name:** a plain-language name such as “More room before responding.”
2. **Development goal:** what the client is trying to become more capable of doing.
3. **Format:** Pause, Perspective, Courage, or Flexibility.
4. **Faculty focus:** the primary faculties being exercised.
5. **Exercises:** the ordered prompts in the routine.
6. **Challenge level:** the current situation difficulty and what can be adjusted.
7. **Session rhythm:** warm-up, main movement, cooldown.
8. **Share:** what the client will send to the coach.
9. **Next progression:** the possible direction for the next workout.

## The first Workout of the Situation

### Name

More room before responding

### Development goal

Notice the urge to leave after criticism and find one more possible response before acting on it.

### Format

Pause

### Primary faculty focus

Emotion awareness, metacognition, judgment, and self-regulation.

### Supporting faculties

Attention, perspective, communication, cognitive flexibility, and learning.

### Session plan

| Physical class language | Room to Respond session |
| --- | --- |
| Warm-up | Bring the review-meeting moment into view. Name what happened, felt, thought, said, did, and wanted. |
| Main movement 1 | Notice the first reaction and the first story. |
| Main movement 2 | Keep, correct, or reject the reflection. |
| Main movement 3 | Run the situation again with one changed condition. |
| Cooldown | Compare what changed, choose what to carry forward, and write the report. |
| Coach review | The coach reads the reflection and assigns the next progression. |

## Progression model

Progression should be visible in the routine, but the product should not claim that a person has “strengthened” a faculty based on one session.

A coach can progress a workout by changing one dimension at a time:

- **Context:** move from a low-stakes situation to a more consequential one;
- **Time:** reduce the available time before choosing a response;
- **Support:** remove or add trusted support after the situation;
- **Complexity:** introduce competing goals or another person's perspective;
- **Expression:** move from private reflection to rehearsing a sentence;
- **Transfer:** ask the client to notice the same faculty in a new setting.

The next routine should say exactly what changed from the previous one.

## Terms that should appear consistently in the prototype

Use:

- workout;
- development goal;
- workout format;
- faculty focus;
- exercise;
- challenge level;
- warm-up;
- main movement;
- cooldown;
- client report;
- coach review;
- next progression.

Avoid switching between unrelated terms such as “journey,” “module,” “reflection thread,” “practice version,” “mental workout,” and “routine” when they refer to the same object. The product can explain the terms once, then use them consistently.
