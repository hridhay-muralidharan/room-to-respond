# Room to Respond — product truth

This is the source of truth for the product meaning. It separates evidence from interpretation and hypotheses so the prototype does not turn an open question into a confident claim.

## The product in one sentence

Room to Respond is a coach-led mental workout product. A therapist or life coach creates a routine for a client, the client examines moments from their life and constructed situations, and both use the resulting reflection to shape the next routine.

## What is inferred from that evidence

Modern life creates many pressures on how people pay attention, regulate emotion, remember, decide, communicate, and adapt. Research usually studies these pressures and faculties separately. Physical gyms provide a useful organising idea: bring exercises together around a person, a goal, and a routine.

Room to Respond applies that organising idea to mental faculties. This is a product synthesis, not a claim that the research already defines a standard mental gym or a validated universal workout routine.

## What remains a hypothesis

These are the hypotheses the product is designed to test:

1. People who already work with therapists, coaches, or digital wellbeing products may value structured development of the faculties they use in everyday situations.
2. Coaches may want a way to turn their judgement about a client's development into repeatable, personalised routines.
3. Clients may find moments from their lives, carefully bounded variations, and reviewable reflections more useful than generic exercises alone.
4. As GenAI automates more execution, some people may place greater value on judgement, taste, self-awareness, communication, emotional regulation, and sense-making. This is a trend to track, not an established market fact.
5. Privacy concerns may currently limit products that need a persistent, intimate model of a person's experiences. Capable offline on-device models may eventually make privacy-first versions more acceptable. The timing and technical threshold need validation.

## Who the product is for

The first product relationship has two participants:

- **Coach:** a therapist, life coach, or comparable professional who chooses a development focus, creates a routine, reviews the client’s reflection, and shapes the next routine.
- **Client:** a person who works through the routine using situations from their own life, corrects the model's reflections, and decides what to share.

The initial target segment is coaches and clients already comfortable with technology-based mental-health, coaching, wellbeing, journaling, meditation, habit, learning, or personal-development products. This is an initial segment hypothesis, not a claim that the whole mental-health market is ready for the product.

Digital mental-health, therapy, coaching, and wellbeing companies are potential distribution partners because they already have coach relationships, client relationships, or both.

## The problem

Coaches have exercises and professional judgement, while clients encounter the moments in which their faculties are exercised. The product opportunity is to connect those two sides through a repeatable routine that can be personalised, practised, reviewed, and continued.

The problem is not simply that clients need more content. The problem is that development work can be difficult to organise across moments from life, multiple faculties, and repeated practice in a way that both coach and client can review.

## The solution

Room to Respond gives the coach a way to:

- choose the faculties and situation they want to work on;
- create a short, personalised mental workout;
- ask the client to think of a moment from their life;
- use a bounded constructed situation to explore another response or possibility;
- receive a reflection that keeps client words, model suggestions, client corrections, and coach feedback distinct;
- use what they learn to create the next workout.

GenAI is the adaptive layer. It helps turn a coach's direction and a client's own words into context-specific questions, reflections, variations, comparisons, and drafts. The coach and client remain the people who set direction, correct meaning, and decide what matters.

## What the current prototype must prove

The prototype is a seeded guided demo for review. It must make one complete coach-client loop understandable without requiring the reviewer to read the repository:

1. The coach gives context for the development focus.
2. The client thinks of a moment from their life.
3. The client reviews and corrects the possible mapping.
4. The client responds to a changed version of the situation.
5. The client compares the two responses and sends one combined report.
6. The coach responds and shapes the next routine.

The prototype is proving the relationship and the workflow. It is not proving clinical efficacy, a universal taxonomy of faculties, or that one routine works for every person.

## Language rules

Use concrete situations: conversations, decisions, work, relationships, conflict, change, and transitions.

Use “develop mental faculties” when describing the product premise. Avoid vague phrases such as “the faculties people use to meet life.”

Label evidence, synthesis, and hypotheses clearly in internal documents. Public copy can be simpler, but must not present a hypothesis as a market fact.

Keep the physical-gym analogy simple: physical gyms organise physical workouts for physical muscles; Room to Respond organises mental workouts for mental faculties.

## Research and product boundaries

The research base supports studying reflection, cognition, emotion, decision-making, memory, motivation, flexibility, foresight, and learning. It does not automatically validate every prompt, transfer effect, or outcome in this product.

Room to Respond is a reflection and exploration product. It does not diagnose, provide crisis support, assess clinical risk, recommend treatment, or replace professional care.
